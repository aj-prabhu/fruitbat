Alice, Bob, Carla, and Dev split a house share last spring, and the money between them got tangled fast.

Alice paid Bob $120 on March 3, covering her half of the electric bill, not the water bill, which Carla had already handled separately. Bob never repaid Alice for a $45 parking ticket he asked her to cover in February, and by June he still owed her that amount, in addition to nothing else, since the $120 was for electric and settled on its own.

Carla lent Dev her car for the weekend of April 12, not her truck, because the truck was in the shop that week. Dev scratched the car's bumper and paid Carla $80 toward the repair, rather than the full $200 estimate, since Carla decided the shop was overcharging and got a second quote for $95 instead. Dev has since paid off that lower amount in full.

Dev owes Carla $40 for a grocery run in May, not $400 — a typo in the group spreadsheet listed it as $400 for two weeks before Alice caught the error and fixed it. Carla has not chased Dev for the $40 yet, and Dev has not offered it either.

Bob sold his old bike to Alice for $150, not $180, because Alice noticed a bent wheel and Bob knocked $30 off rather than argue about it. Alice paid that $150 in two installments of $75 each, on April 20 and May 4.

Carla paid the March internet bill of $60 by herself, instead of splitting it four ways, because she signed up for the plan under her own name and didn't want to deal with reimbursement math for a bill that small. No one has offered to pay her back, and she says she no longer expects it.

By the end of May, only two debts were still open in the house: Bob owed Alice $45, not $120, and Dev owed Carla $40, not $400. Alice and Carla owed no one anything. Bob had not repaid the $45 by the end of June either, and Dev still had not offered the $40, though Carla says she will not chase either amount before the lease ends on August 31.
